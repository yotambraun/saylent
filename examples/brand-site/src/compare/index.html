Kestrel and the alternatives

Nobody buys monitoring because they enjoy it. Teams arrive here from one of three places, and it is worth being honest about which of them Kestrel is actually better than.

Coming from the hosted incumbents

The large hosted monitoring suites do far more than Kestrel does. They correlate traces, they profile your runtime, they hold years of logs, and if your company has a platform team whose job is to run that, they are a reasonable place to be. The problem small teams describe to us is not capability. It is that the bill grows with things they did not choose, the setup asks for decisions they are not ready to make, and the on-call configuration ends up being understood by one person.

Kestrel is smaller on purpose. It answers one question, is the thing up and is anyone being told, and it tries to answer that question well from four regions rather than answering ten questions from one. If you need distributed tracing, you should not buy Kestrel. If you have a tracing product already and still find out about outages from customers, that is the gap Kestrel fills.

Coming from an open source stack you run yourself

A self hosted metrics and alerting stack is genuinely good, and it is free until you count the afternoons. The failure mode we see is the one nobody plans for: the monitoring runs in the same infrastructure as the thing it monitors, so the outage that matters most is the outage nobody is paged for. Alert rules drift, the person who wrote them changes team, and the certificate on the alert manager expires quietly.

Kestrel runs outside your infrastructure, which is the whole point of an external check. It does not replace your internal metrics. It sits next to them and answers from the outside, the way your customers experience the service.

Coming from a cron job and a shell script

Plenty of teams start with a script that curls an endpoint and sends an email. It works, and it is the right first step. It stops working the day the script host has a network problem and sends three hundred emails, or the day it silently stops running and everything looks fine because no news is good news.

Kestrel is the version of that script with the boring parts finished: agreement between regions before anyone is woken, escalation when the first person does not answer, a record of what happened, and a status page customers can read instead of writing to support.

Where Kestrel is the wrong answer

If you need log search, if you need application performance monitoring, if you are required to keep monitoring data inside your own network, or if your compliance programme needs a vendor with a dedicated account team, Kestrel is not the right tool and we will say so on a call rather than let you find out in month three.
