A check only counts when two regions agree

Most false alarms are network weather, not your software. Kestrel runs every check from Frankfurt, Virginia, Singapore and São Paulo. One region failing is a note in the timeline. Two regions failing within the same window is an incident, and only then does anyone get woken up.

You can tighten that rule per check. A payment endpoint can page on the first failure. A marketing page can wait for three.

check api.kestrel.example/healthz fra-1 timeout 10.0s iad-1 200 OK 184ms sin-1 200 OK 206ms gru-1 200 OK 311ms result 1 of 4 regions failing action logged, no page sent

Alerts that reach a person, not a channel nobody reads

Every check belongs to a rotation. Kestrel notifies the engineer on call by push, SMS and email, waits five minutes for an acknowledgement, then escalates to the next person and the team channel. Quiet hours apply to warnings, never to outages.

Alerts carry the last response body, the response time trend for the hour before the failure, and the last deploy your team recorded through the API, so the first question on the call is usually already answered.

A status page you never have to remember to update

Turn on a status page and Kestrel publishes it at your own domain with your colours and your wording. It updates itself from the same checks that page your team, so it can never quietly disagree with reality.

Customers subscribe by email or webhook. When you write an incident note it goes to the page and to every subscriber at once, which is usually enough to stop the support queue filling up while you work.

How a check runs

Four steps, the same for an HTTP endpoint, a TCP port, a cron heartbeat or an SSL certificate.

  1. Kestrel requests your endpoint from four regions

    Every 30 seconds on paid plans, every 5 minutes on the free plan. You choose the method, headers, body and the response rule that counts as healthy.

  2. The result is compared across regions

    Kestrel keeps the status code, the response time, the response body up to 8KB, and which region saw it. Disagreement between regions is recorded rather than hidden.

  3. An incident opens when your rule is met

    The default rule is two regions failing in the same 60 second window. The incident collects everything Kestrel knows, including your last recorded deploy.

  4. People and pages are told in the same second

    The on-call engineer is paged, the status page changes, subscribers are notified. When the checks recover the incident closes itself and the timeline stays for the review.
