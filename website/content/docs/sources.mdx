---
title: Sources & opportunities
description: Where the answers come from, who they name, and which off-site pages are worth winning. Counts with receipts, built from the run's own data.
---

A run already stores every answer, its verdict, its citations and the cited
pages it read. Sources & opportunities reads that data three ways:

- **Citation share**: which domains and pages the answers cite, per engine and
  answer surface
- **Share of voice**: how many answers name you, and how many name each rival
- **Off-site opportunities**: domains that are neither yours nor a rival's,
  cited in answers that name a rival and not you

It makes no model call and no fetch of its own, so it costs nothing. Every
number is a count, and every count lists the exact answers behind it, by
question and engine, so you can open each one.

## What counts as an answer here

- An answer is counted **once per question and engine**: the answer the
  report shows, never the extra sampled draws. That is why every receipt
  opens.
- Only answered rows count. A surface question that was
  [not shown](/docs/surfaces#when-there-is-no-ai-answer) is not an answer.
- **Names you** means the judge's verdict says your brand is present.
  **Recommends** means its mention type is "recommended". A rival is
  **named** when the verdict lists it among the brands the answer prefers or
  recommends.
- **Names a rival and not you** means the answer was judged, your brand is
  absent, and at least one rival is named.
- **Branded questions** ("is your brand any good") are left out of the
  opportunities. They are about you, not about the category.

## Citation share

For every cited domain:

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| Citations | Citation rows on this domain, across the counted answers |
| Share | Its citations divided by every citation in the run |
| Per engine | Citations by each engine and surface |
| Answers | Distinct answers citing it |
| Name you | Answers citing it that name you |
| Name a rival, not you | Answers citing it that name a rival and not you |
| Top pages | Its most cited pages (up to five), and for each page we read, whether you and which rivals are on it |
| Type | Its domain type, and the rule that set it ([below](#domain-types)) |

The host is the real source: `www.` is stripped, Internet Archive snapshots
count for the site they copy, and a Gemini grounding redirect link counts for
the site its citation names. The run also gets a per-type total (citations and domains per
type) and, per engine, its answers, citations, domains and top five domains.

## Share of voice

Counts, not percentages: the answers naming you, the answers naming each rival
(up to twelve rivals), and how many answers recommend you, each split by
engine and each with its receipts. A rival has no "recommended" count, because
the verdict does not separate "recommends" from "prefers" for other brands.

This counts mentions in the answers we drew. It is not market share.

## Off-site opportunities

An off-site opportunity is a domain that:

1. is not your site and not a rival's site (by its [domain type](#domain-types))
2. is cited by at least one answer that names a rival and not you
3. on a question that is not branded
4. where you were not found on any page we read on it

They are ranked by **evidence**: the number of distinct such answers that
cite the domain. Ties go to the domain cited by more engines, then the one with
more citations, then by host name. There are no weights and no score.

Each opportunity carries:

- its type, and the rule that set it
- the engines and questions behind it
- the rivals named in those answers, most often first
- what the pages we read on it show: rivals are there and you are not, you are
  not there, or no page on it was read
- the top cited page on it, the one to pitch first
- one plain sentence saying why it is listed, the same sentence in every view
- receipts: the question, the engine, the cited URLs on that domain, the
  rivals the answer named with the reason it gave, and (in `saylent sources`
  and the MCP tool) a short quote of the answer

A run keeps up to twelve opportunities, with up to six receipts each.

## Domain types

| Type | Label |
|---|---|
| `own_site` | Your site |
| `rival_site` | Rival's site |
| `review_site` | Review site |
| `forum` | Forum / community |
| `docs` | Docs / help |
| `news` | News |
| `marketplace` | Marketplace |
| `reference` | Reference |
| `video` | Video |
| `social` | Social |
| `editorial` | Blog / editorial |
| `other` | Other |

A short list of rules decides each type. The first rule that matches wins, and
every view shows the rule that fired.

| # | Rule | Type | Shown as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your override for this host or a parent domain | the type you set | "your override" |
| 2 | Your domain, or a subdomain of it | `own_site` | "your domain" |
| 3 | A known review site (`g2.com`, `capterra.com`, `trustpilot.com`, ...) | `review_site` | "known review site" |
| 4 | A known community site (`reddit.com`, `stackoverflow.com`, ...) | `forum` | "known community site" |
| 5 | A known reference site (`wikipedia.org`, `wikidata.org`, `britannica.com`) | `reference` | "known reference site" |
| 6 | A known video site (`youtube.com`, `vimeo.com`, ...) | `video` | "known video site" |
| 7 | A known marketplace or app store (`apps.apple.com`, `play.google.com`, any `amazon.*`, ...) | `marketplace` | "known marketplace" |
| 8 | A known social network (`linkedin.com`, `x.com`, ...) | `social` | "known social network" |
| 9 | The host is named after a rival: a configured competitor, or a rival the answers named, spelled out whole | `rival_site` | "named after" the rival |
| 10 | The host contains a configured competitor's name, or a rival's domain redirects there | `rival_site` | the name it contains, or "a ... domain redirects here" |
| 11 | A subdomain pattern: `docs.`, `help.`, `support.`, `api.` and similar; `community.`, `forum.`; `news.`, `press.`; `blog.`, `resources.`; `marketplace.`, `apps.`, `store.` | `docs`, `forum`, `news`, `editorial`, `marketplace` | "docs/help subdomain" and so on |
| 12 | What its cited pages look like: when at least half its citations point to one kind of page (best-of listicles or comparisons, docs, news, review listings, community threads, reference entries, videos) | `editorial`, `docs`, `news`, `review_site`, `forum`, `reference`, `video` | for example "3 of 4 citations point to best-of listicles" |
| 13 | Nothing matched | `other` | "no rule matched" |

The host lists live in `packages/engine/src/classify.ts` and
`packages/engine/src/sources.ts`.

## Correct a type

When a rule gets a domain wrong, override it. The override always wins.

In `saylent.config.json`:

```json
{
  "sources": {
    "domainTypes": {
      "softwarereviews.example": "review_site",
      "blog.rival.example": "rival_site"
    }
  }
}
```

A parent domain covers its subdomains, and the most specific entry wins. The
value must be one of the twelve types above. `saylent audit` and
`saylent verify` apply it, and the run stores the overrides it was computed
with (`sources.overrides` in `run.json`), so a re-render reproduces it.

In the app, the brand's run options page has a **Source types** editor. It
changes how every report for that brand reads its stored answers, never what a
run asks, so it never starts a new baseline.

## Where it shows up

| Where | What you get |
|---|---|
| `report.html` and the app's full report view | Section 04, **Sources & opportunities**. Every count opens the answers behind it. Site checks, fixes and method are now sections 05, 06 and 07 |
| `report.md` | A `## Sources & opportunities` section |
| `saylent audit` finish summary | A `Sources` line and an `Off-site` line |
| `saylent sources` | The full view in the terminal, or JSON ([below](#saylent-sources)) |
| The MCP server | The `sources` tool, and `sources_summary` in the `audit` result ([The MCP server](/docs/mcp)) |
| The app's brand page | **Sources over time**, across the last six finished runs ([below](#history)) |

The finish summary prints two lines, counts only:

```
  Sources   <n> citations · <n> domains · most cited: <host> (<n>, <type>)
  Off-site  <n> opportunities · first: <host>, in <n> answers that name a rival and not you
```

## `saylent sources`

```bash
# everything for one run, as text
npx saylent sources ./example.com/2026-11-02/run.json

# only the opportunities that Google AI Overviews cited
npx saylent sources ./example.com/2026-11-02/run.json --view opportunities --engine google_ai_overview

# one domain: its pages and every answer citing it
npx saylent sources ./example.com/2026-11-02/run.json --domain g2.com

# the same JSON the MCP sources tool returns
npx saylent sources ./example.com/2026-11-02/run.json --json
```

| Flag | What it does |
|---|---|
| `--view <name>` | `summary`, `opportunities`, `domains`, `voice` or `all` (default: `all`) |
| `--engine <id>` | Only rows cited by this engine or surface |
| `--type <type>` | Only domains of this type (one of the twelve above) |
| `--domain <host>` | One domain: its pages and every answer citing it |
| `--limit <n>` | Rows per list (default 10, max 50) |
| `--json` | Print the same JSON the MCP `sources` tool returns |

It reads the bundle and nothing else: no network, no model call, $0. A folder
works in place of the `run.json` path.

## History

Pass two or more bundles of the same brand and `saylent sources` prints the
history instead:

```bash
npx saylent sources ./example.com/2026-10-02/run.json ./example.com/2026-11-02/run.json
```

Per run: the citations and the number of off-site opportunities. Per domain:
its citations in each run, and how many runs it was an off-site opportunity in.
Per brand: the answers naming you and the top rivals in each run. Bundles of
different brands are refused. With `--json` it prints the history as JSON.

The app's brand page shows the same thing as **Sources over time**, over the
last six finished runs: citations per domain per run, "opportunity in N of M
runs", and named counts per run. The receipts live in each run's report.

## In `run.json`

`run.json` has an optional `sources` block with `schema_version: 1`:

| Key | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `totals` | answers, answers with citations, citations, domains, and answers that name a rival and not you (non-branded) |
| `engines` | Per engine: answers, citations, domains, top domains |
| `domains` | Every cited domain, most cited first, with the fields in [Citation share](#citation-share) |
| `byType` | Citations and domains per type |
| `voice` | You and the rivals: named, recommended (you only), per engine, receipts |
| `opportunities` | Ranked, with evidence, engines, rivals, page check, reason, top page and receipts |
| `overrides` | The type overrides it was computed with |

Older bundles have no `sources` block and still read unchanged: the report,
`saylent sources` and the MCP tool compute it from the bundle's own rows with
the same function. A block with a `schema_version` this build does not know is
recomputed the same way. The MCP tool says which happened (`recomputed`).

## What it does not claim

- A citation shows where an answer pointed. It does not show that the page
  caused the answer.
- An opportunity is a place rivals are cited and you are not. Being there is
  no promise that the answer changes.
- Share of voice counts the answers we drew, not the market.
- Domain types come from short rules, not a model. Each one shows its rule,
  and you can override it.
