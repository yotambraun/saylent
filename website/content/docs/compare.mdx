Looking for how to compare your own brand against a rival inside the app? That
is a different feature: see [Compare against a
rival](/docs/tour#compare-rivals). This page is about Saylent next to other
products.

<Callout title="Which tool?">
[promptbeacon](https://github.com/yotambraun/promptbeacon) is for developers and open-source maintainers: does AI recommend your library or dev product — a number in CI and a README badge. Saylent is for brand and website teams: an audit of what AI assistants say about your site, with the answers and cited pages as receipts, plus fixes for your site.
</Callout>

Saylent is one shape of tool in a category that also has well-funded hosted
products, and a growing number of open-source projects. This page states
what is structurally different, each hosted product's entry price as its own
pricing page shows it, and what the open-source trackers say about
themselves, all as of November 2026 and each with a link. It does not rate
any product's quality - see [the methodology](/docs/methodology) for what
Saylent itself does and does not claim.

## What is structurally different

| | Saylent | Hosted tools (Profound, Peec, Otterly, Scrunch, AthenaHQ, Semrush AI Visibility, Ahrefs Brand Radar, HubSpot AEO) |
|---|---|---|
| Open source | Yes - Apache-2.0, this repository | No - proprietary, closed-source products |
| Runs on your own provider keys | Yes - every dollar goes to OpenAI/Anthropic/Google/Perplexity directly, never through us ([What it costs](/docs/costs)) | No - usage runs on the vendor's own infrastructure and account, priced into their subscription |
| Self-hostable | Yes - CLI on your machine, or the full app on your own server, Docker, or your own Vercel/Supabase project ([Deploy the app](/docs/self-host)) | No - hosted SaaS only |

## What the hosted tools do that Saylent does not (yet)

This is the honest gap, not a hedge:

- **Daily tracking, run for you.** The hosted tools re-ask their prompts
  every day on their own infrastructure. Saylent asks when you run it: by
  hand, from cron or CI ([scheduled checks](/docs/scheduled-checks)), or on
  the app's optional weekly-verify and monthly-audit schedules
  (`FLAG_SCHEDULED_RUNS`, off by default), each run billed to your own keys.
  It keeps every run you save and compares them ([verify and
  history](/docs/verify-and-history)), and since 0.2.0 sends
  [alerts](/docs/alerts) when the answers move between two runs and a
  weekly digest; there is no alert between runs, because nothing is asked
  between runs.
- **Answers as the consumer apps show them.** Saylent asks the providers'
  official APIs, and reads Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode and Microsoft
  Copilot through your own SerpApi key ([answer surfaces](/docs/surfaces)).
  It does not drive chatgpt.com or the other consumer apps, which can
  answer differently from the API ([methodology](/docs/methodology)).
- **A shared workspace.** One deployment supports as many user accounts as you
  like, each isolated from the others by row-level security, with one operator
  who owns the keys and the bill. What it does not have is a workspace those
  users share: no common brand list, and no switching between client accounts
  from one login.
- **Seats, roles and billing.** Everyone who signs up is a user; there is no
  per-seat role model, no seat billing, and no plan tier.
- **Vendor support.** A hosted subscription buys a support relationship;
  an open-source project's support is public GitHub issues and (where
  enabled) Discussions.

## Open-source alternatives

Saylent is not the only open-source project here. These describe themselves
as follows in their own repositories (read 2026-11-02); each is worth
reading in full before you choose. The largest difference between them is
where the answers come from: an official API, a search-results provider, or
the consumer app itself.

| Project | License | What its README says it does | Where answers come from |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Elmo](https://github.com/elmohq/elmo) | MIT | AEO/GEO platform: visibility per prompt and model over time, share of voice, sentiment and position, citations, query fan-out, opportunities, shareable reports, a REST API, SDKs and an MCP server. Self-hosted with Docker Compose; a hosted Elmo Cloud also exists | ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and AI Overviews, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot scraped from the consumer apps through a Cloro key; Claude, Grok and others through model APIs (OpenRouter) |
| [Limelit Open](https://github.com/limelit-co/open) | Apache-2.0 | Self-hosted AI visibility tracking in one Go binary with SQLite, MCP-first, bring your own keys: visibility and share of voice, citation classes, a prompt-by-engine grid, a scheduler, JSON/CSV export. Describes itself as pre-release | ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overview, Google AI Mode and Bing Copilot, with your own keys; some providers not yet built |
| [OneGlanse](https://github.com/oneglanse/oneglanse) | MIT | AI visibility tracker: saves answers and citations, whether and how a brand is recommended, competing brands, cited domains, changes across runs. Runs locally or self-hosted with Docker Compose | The AI products' websites, with your own logged-in accounts, for ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude and Google AI Overview; scores come from an analysis model you connect |
| [geo-aeo-tracker](https://github.com/danishashko/geo-aeo-tracker) | MIT | Self-hosted visibility tracker: a 0-100 visibility score, citation opportunities, competitor battlecards, a site audit, scheduled runs and drift alerts, workspaces. Data in the browser by default, optional sync to your own Supabase | Bright Data's AI scraper for ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot and Google AI Mode; OpenRouter and the Gemini API for analysis |

Saylent's own choices, for the same comparison: official provider APIs for
ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity, SerpApi for the three answer
surfaces, a cross-family judge, a site-access check, drafted fixes tied to
their evidence, and a receipt behind every number. It does not scrape the
consumer apps.

## Why this page exists

The published paid entry tiers above run from about $29 to $300 a month, and several already offer a free
one-shot check as their own lead-generation ([Ahrefs' free AI visibility
checker](https://ahrefs.com/ai-visibility-checker) is a "no signup" example).
An "open-source alternative to an expensive incumbent" story does not
describe this category the way it describes, say, Plausible vs Google
Analytics - Saylent's reason to exist is different: your own keys, your own
data, self-hostable, and the receipts behind every score, not a lower price
for the same closed product.
